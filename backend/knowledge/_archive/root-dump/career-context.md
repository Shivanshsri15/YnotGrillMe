# 14. Career Decisions and Current Direction

## 14.1 Building on backend engineering

My professional background is broad, covering mobile development, full-stack SaaS, backend processing, infrastructure, and AI-powered features.

During career planning, I expressed concern that this breadth might make my profile look insufficiently specialized in a competitive job market.

I considered learning another backend language, including Go, Rust, or Python, to develop a stronger technical niche.

The concern was not that I lacked experience. It was that my experience covered many areas without necessarily establishing a single, obvious specialization.

## 14.2 Why not learn every new language immediately?

The career discussion considered the opportunity cost of starting another language while I was already preparing for interviews, practicing DSA daily, and studying AWS.

Learning a new language would require time that could otherwise be spent improving existing backend skills, strengthening system-design knowledge, practicing interviews, or applying for jobs.

The conclusion was to avoid learning Go and Rust simultaneously merely for differentiation.

Python was considered the most immediately complementary option because it could support backend development, automation, and AI engineering.

Go remained a possible later specialization for backend infrastructure and platform engineering if the roles I targeted justified it.

Rust was considered less immediately aligned with my near-term goal of general backend employability unless I developed a genuine interest in systems programming or found roles where it was a clear requirement.

These were career recommendations discussed during planning, rather than completed learning milestones.

## 14.3 AI engineering

I expressed that, although I had worked on AI-related features and projects, I considered my AI engineering knowledge to be at a beginner level.

That distinction is important.

I have practical exposure to RAG, vector search, LLM APIs, and multi-agent workflows, but I do not want my professional profile to imply expertise beyond what I can explain and defend in an interview.

My intended direction is to continue learning AI engineering while building on my existing backend experience.

## 14.4 AWS and system design

I had started preparing for the AWS Solutions Architect Associate certification but acknowledged that I was not studying consistently.

The career discussion therefore emphasized making the AWS work more structured and practical rather than continually adding new learning goals.

System design was another area I wanted to develop after strengthening my immediate interview preparation.

## 14.5 Current preparation priorities

The priorities recorded during the career discussion were:

1. **DSA:** Continue daily problem-solving and interview preparation.
2. **Development interviews:** Improve my ability to explain JavaScript, TypeScript, Node.js, NestJS, APIs, databases, and architecture.
3. **AWS:** Resume consistent preparation, supported by practical labs.
4. **Python and backend development:** Add Python when the interview-preparation routine is stable.
5. **System design:** Develop a deeper understanding of databases, caching, queues, distributed systems, reliability, and service architecture.
6. **Job applications and outreach:** Continue applying rather than waiting until every learning goal is complete.

The overall direction was to strengthen my backend engineering foundation and use AI as a complementary capability, rather than immediately changing my professional identity to an entirely different specialization.
